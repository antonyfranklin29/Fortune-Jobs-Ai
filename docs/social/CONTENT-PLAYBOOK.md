# Fortune Jobs AI — Instagram content playbook

Audience: college students, new graduates, early-career professionals and international students in the U.S. They are smart, busy and skeptical. They follow LinkedIn, Notion, Linear, Duolingo and Headspace — that is the bar.

Goal of every post: earn a save or a share by being genuinely useful, and give one clear reason to visit fortunejobsai.com. We are a career tool, not a store. **No "job of the day", no deal-style hype, no stock-photo-with-a-headline posts.**

## Voice
- Calm, expert, encouraging. A senior career coach who respects the reader's time.
- Specific over generic: "Recruiters spend about 7 seconds on a first résumé scan" beats "Stand out to recruiters!"
- Never exaggerate. No "guaranteed", "dream job", "#1", "skip the middleman", "straight from employers". No fake testimonials, no discounts, no invented stats — every number must be real and sourced (our own catalogue counts, or a named public source in the caption).
- One idea per post. Headline ≤ 7 words. Body text on the image ≤ 25 words total.

## Content pillars (rotate in this order; never the same pillar twice in a row)
1. **Why Fortune Jobs AI** — the problem we solve, said like a human. E.g. "Job boards show you everything. We show you what fits." / "Your résumé, read the way an ATS reads it."
2. **How it works (product, 3 steps)** — a clean phone mockup of a real screen + 3 short numbered steps. Topics: Quick Match, Live Search, free ATS score, AI search ("describe the job you want"), application tracker, work-authorization filter, job pages by state/city, saved jobs.
3. **Career craft (useful advice)** — résumé, ATS, interviews, LinkedIn, follow-ups, salary research, first-job decisions. Carousel-style single images: a strong claim + 3 crisp tips.
4. **Get started / sign up** — what you get with a free account in 30 seconds (save jobs, track applications, free ATS score); one soft CTA: "Create your free account → fortunejobsai.com".
5. **Market insight (real data)** — one honest number from our live catalogue with context, e.g. "331 open Software Engineer roles in Texas this week" + what it means for a job seeker. Always "as of <date>", never stale.
6. **International students (honest)** — how to read sponsorship/OPT language in a posting, what our work-authorization filter does (finds postings that *mention* it; it doesn't promise sponsorship).

Ratio per 2 weeks (28 posts): Why FJA 4 · How it works 7 · Career craft 8 · Get started 3 · Market insight 3 · International students 3.

## Design system (every post)
- Canvas 1080×1350. Generous margins (≥ 96 px). Strict grid; one focal point.
- Colors: Navy #10243B (primary background or text), Gold #D4A843 (one accent only — a word, a rule, a number), Cream #FFF7E6, White, and one muted slate #5B6B7F for secondary text. No gradients except a subtle navy vignette.
- Type: one geometric/grotesk sans (e.g. Inter, Satoshi, Plus Jakarta Sans). Headline 72–96 pt bold, tight leading; body 34–40 pt regular. Max two weights per post.
- Imagery, in order of preference: (a) real product screenshots in a clean phone/laptop frame; (b) editorial, candid photography of real-looking students/young professionals in natural light (no cheesy handshakes, no thumbs-up, no obviously staged groups); (c) simple custom icons/illustration in brand colors. Never clip-art, never cluttered collages.
- Brand lock-up bottom-left on every post: small logo + "fortunejobsai.com" in slate or cream, never larger than 5% of the canvas.
- Accessibility: contrast ≥ 4.5:1, no text over busy photo areas.
- Consistency: posts should look like one family on the grid — alternate dark (navy) and light (cream) backgrounds.

## Captions
- First line is a hook that works alone (≤ 12 words). Then 2–4 short lines of value. Then one CTA. Then ≤ 5 relevant hashtags (#jobsearch #newgrad #careeradvice #resumetips #internationalstudents as fits).
- Sound like a person: no emojis walls; at most 2 emojis.

## Quality bar — check before export
1. Would a design lead at LinkedIn or Notion post this? If not, redo.
2. Is it useful even if the reader never visits our site?
3. Is every claim true today?
4. Readable in 2 seconds on a phone?
5. Brand lock-up present, nothing else competing with the headline?
