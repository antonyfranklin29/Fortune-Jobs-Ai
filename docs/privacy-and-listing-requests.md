# Handling privacy, safety and listing requests

Operational draft, October 24, 2026. The mailbox is `info@fortunejobsai.com`. Assign a named owner and test this process before public launch. Do not store copies of résumés, identity documents or complaint details in Git, public issue trackers or routine chat.

1. Check Inbox and Junk each business day. Record a restricted case number, receipt date, request type, account identifier if known, country/state, due date and current owner. Do not put message contents in a public tracker. A privacy request does not need special legal words to count.
2. Triage immediately: account access/export/correction/deletion, newsletter unsubscribe, suspicious job, copyright/content complaint, accessibility barrier or security incident. Stop marketing to an unsubscribed address through the preference system; a mailbox acknowledgement alone is not suppression.
3. For access, export or deletion, use an existing signed-in control where it works. For an email request, verify the requester proportionately through the account email or sign-in; do not request a government ID by default, and do not disclose account data to an unverified correspondent. Record what was verified, not the secret used to verify it. Confirm which country/state's rules and deadline apply; UK access requests normally have a one-month response window.
4. Identify all applicable stores: Supabase Auth, profile, saved jobs, search history, private résumé objects and extracted text, public avatar object, newsletter preference/delivery records, employer membership and review history, Live Search admission data, backups and provider logs. Account deletion must not silently delete another company's shared records. Tell the requester if any data must be retained for a specific lawful reason and when backup copies expire. Do not claim a request is complete until the actual deletion/export steps and exceptions have been checked.
5. For a suspicious or closed job, capture its URL, source and report time; hide or close it promptly while investigating impersonation, application-fee requests, discriminatory wording or unsafe links. Check whether a provider or employer feed must also be notified. Reply with the outcome without revealing a reporter's private details.
6. For a security incident, restrict access and preserve evidence, identify what data and people may be affected, engage the operator and qualified counsel immediately, determine notification duties and deadlines by jurisdiction, and record the decisions. Do not delete evidence or promise that no data was exposed before investigation.
7. Close each case with the actions, date, reviewer and response recorded in the restricted log. Recheck failed or partial actions. Periodically audit the queue and the newsletter suppression list.

Sources: [ICO subject-access guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/subject-access-requests/a-guide-to-subject-access/), [FTC data-breach response guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/data-breach-response-guide-business), [FTC job-scam guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/07/looking-job-spot-scams).
